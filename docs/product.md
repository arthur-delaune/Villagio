# Product: architecture & priorities

## Product principles

1. **Local before national.** Every screen starts from *your* club and *your* neighbourhood.
2. **The child, not the item.** Parents think "what does Léa need for judo this year?", not "size-130 gi".
3. **Trust without friction.** Community context (same club, same school) does the work that ratings and escrow do elsewhere.
4. **Seasonal by design.** The product follows the rhythm of the school and sports year.

## Core user journeys

**Seller (end of season)**
`Pick the child's activity` → `photo + size + condition` → `publish to my club and neighbourhood` → `meet after training` → `get paid`

**Buyer (start of season)**
`Join my club's space` → `filter by activity, size and distance` → `buy a single item or a pack` → `pick up locally`

**Club (partner)**
`Claim the club space` → `share the link with members` → `run an end-of-season kit swap` → `see activity on a dashboard`

## Validation approach

A **concierge MVP** comes before any heavy build: a landing page, structured forms and manual matching. It tests two hypotheses at the lowest possible cost:

- **H1:** parents will list end-of-season gear when asked through their club
- **H2:** buyers prefer a local, club-based pick-up to a cheaper shipped item

Clubs that prove both hypotheses become the launch cohort for the marketplace MVP.
